Why does a key turn?

    A lock opens only when every pin splits exactly at the shear line.

    Sample interaction · replace

    More on this
    • Each pin stack is a key pin under a driver pin, pushed down by a spring.
    • The right key lifts every stack until the gap between its two pins sits on the shear line.
    • Too low or too high, and a driver pin still bridges the line, holding the plug in the housing.

    What stops a wrong key?

    A wrong key leaves one pin across the line, and the plug stays put.

    Interaction goes here

    Why one pin is enough
    • The plug turns only when every stack is clear of the line, so the other four can be perfect and it still holds.
    • A key that is close, but not exact, feels almost right and fails the same way.

    How many keys are there?

    Five pins with ten heights each make 100,000 possible keys.

    The numbers behind 100,000

    Each pin multiplies the count by its number of heights, so every extra pin adds a zero.

    Where is the weak spot?

    Sloppy tolerances are the weak spot: the more slack, the easier it is to fake a key.

    Interaction goes here

    What the slack costs
    • Slack lets a wrong cut still lift a pin close enough to the line.
    • Caveat: real locks do not use every one of the 100,000 keys, because neighbouring heights cannot differ too much.
